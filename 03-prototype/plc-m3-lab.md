## Name the situation
The situation involves a Head of Data Science, an individual contributor who joined the team approximately five months ago to replace the previous Head of Data Science. During his first few months, he worked to understand the existing work in progress, including both tactical delivery and the longer-term strategic direction of the AI Assistant. As he became more familiar with the solution, he identified that the underlying architecture was behind what he believed was required to support the product’s current and future evolution. He has since raised concerns about the relationship between the product vision, roadmap, and technical architecture, stating that “the vision is not wrong; it is not adequate to the mechanism underneath it” and that “we are not delivering against this roadmap; we are delivering against a roadmap for a different architecture, and that architecture is now in progress.” He has consequently proposed that the existing roadmap should be voided and rebuilt to reflect the architecture now being developed. These concerns have emerged over the past several months as he has gained greater context on the existing solution and its technical foundations.

## Make a diagnosis
This is primarily an ownership and accountability gap around technical leadership and architecture, rather than a problem with the product vision or roadmap.
Why: My frustration is with the behaviour because technical architecture should evolve to enable the agreed product strategy and desired outcomes; identifying architectural limitations is valuable, but using those limitations as a reason to invalidate the product roadmap shifts the constraint onto the product vision rather than taking ownership of how the technical foundation needs to evolve to support it.

## Write your opening line
- **One next action I will take in the next two weeks is:** I will facilitate a focused alignment session with the Head of Data Science and relevant technical leads to explicitly separate product intent from architectural implementation, agree which architectural gaps genuinely constrain the roadmap, and establish clear ownership for defining how the architecture needs to evolve to support the agreed product outcomes - rather than redesigning the product roadmap around the current technical architecture.
- **The first sentence of the conversation I need to have is:** I’d like us to align on the relationship between the product roadmap and the technical architecture, specifically how we ensure the architecture evolves to enable the agreed product outcomes while making any genuine technical constraints explicit.

## AI role-play
- **After you step out: what did the role-play change about how you will open this conversation for real?:** I would actually avoid opening with roadmap vs architecture.
That framing invites debate over which should dominate.
Instead, I'd open around shared responsibilities. For example:
'I'd like us to align on how Product and Technical Leadership work together as the AI Assistant evolves. I don't think we're far apart on the technical realities - we both recognise the architecture needs to evolve. Where I think we're less aligned is how we distinguish the product outcomes we're trying to achieve from the architectural work needed to enable them, and who owns defining that path.'

## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** Our mission
The AI Assistant team owns the strategic capability that makes MyCF the fastest and most trusted operational interface for fleet managers. We are accountable for increasing customer retention, Net Revenue Retention and Customer Lifetime Value by making conversational interaction the primary workflow for operational decision-making.
We own
The AI Assistant team owns end-to-end decisions for the core conversational capability, including:
AI Assistant product vision, strategy and roadmap.
Conversational user experience and interaction model.
AI quality, trust and answer correctness.
Retrieval and grounding against trusted MyCF and approved third-party data.
Explainability and evidence citations.
AI evaluation framework and quality scorecards.
Enterprise governance for latency, hallucination, safety and cost.
Operational intelligence capabilities delivered through conversation.
Cross-product AI platform capabilities that create strategic differentiation.
Measurement of behavioural adoption and commercial impact of AI.
We are accountable for ensuring every AI capability strengthens MyCF's position as the authoritative operational system.
- **What is out of scope.:** The AI Assistant team does not own:
Customer onboarding journeys.
Product activation programmes.
Retention campaigns and lifecycle communications.
General Growth experimentation outside AI experiences.
Product areas unrelated to conversational intelligence.
Commodity AI infrastructure (owned by Platform/Architecture).
Foundation models and vendor selection beyond agreed architectural standards.
These teams remain accountable for their own business outcomes and customer experiences.

- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** Cross-boundary decisions
When work spans multiple product areas:
Step 1 — Working-level alignment (within 3 working days)
Relevant PMs meet to agree:

success metrics
If agreement is reached, the accountable owner proceeds.
Step 2 — Product leadership review (within 5 working days)
If agreement cannot be reached, the issue is escalated to:
Head of Product
AI Assistant PM
Relevant PMs
The discussion focuses on:
customer value
strategic alignment
measurable business outcomes
impact on other teams
A decision is recorded and communicated within two working days.
Step 3 — Executive escalation (only if required)
Escalation occurs only when the decision changes:
strategic priorities
quarterly commitments
funding
organisational ownership
The final decision is made by the Product Leadership Team.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** Guiding principles
When making trade-offs we will prioritise:
Customer outcomes over feature ownership.
Product strategy over local optimisation.
Long-term trust over short-term engagement.
Shared success metrics over team-specific metrics.
Evidence from customer behaviour over opinion.
Decisions made closest to the work whenever possible, escalating only when necessary.
