## Name how you decide, in your own world
- **Your company context. Name your company, your product, your market, and the main constraints you operate under.:** Company: Michelin Connected Fleet (MCF)
Product: AI Assistant within the MyCF Platform
Market: B2B fleet management and connected mobility, serving commercial fleet operators across Europe. Our customers manage cars, vans and HGVs and rely on MyCF for operational decisions, compliance, safety and cost optimisation.
Main constraints:
We only create value where customers have sufficient proprietary fleet data inside MyCF.
Every answer must be evidence-backed, explainable and trusted; a confident but incorrect answer destroys adoption.
AI capabilities must operate within enterprise security, privacy and regulatory requirements.
Engineering capacity is shared across multiple platform initiatives, so every investment competes with core platform work.
The AI Assistant depends on the quality and maturity of underlying platform services, data models and APIs, creating constant trade-offs between product delivery and architectural evolution.
- **Strategy. How do you tell a real strategy from a list of goals in your world?:** In my world, a strategy is not a vision statement or a roadmap. It is a deliberate choice about where we will win, where we will not compete, and why those choices create a durable business advantage.
For the MyCF AI Assistant, the strategy is not "build an AI assistant" or "increase adoption." Those are goals. The strategy is to transform MyCF from a system customers occasionally use into the operational system they depend on every day by making conversation the primary interface to fleet intelligence. We deliberately focus on existing MyCF customers because proprietary fleet data creates differentiation that generic AI cannot replicate. We sequence broad cross-platform capabilities before specialised workflows because platform-wide daily usage increases retention across the installed customer base faster than solving niche use cases. Every roadmap decision should strengthen that strategic position; if a feature increases usage but does not move us closer to becoming indispensable in customers' daily operational workflows, it is not advancing the strategy.

- **Financials. How do you judge whether a bet is worth making, and what would make you kill it?:** I judge product bets by whether they create long-term enterprise value rather than short-term feature adoption. For the AI Assistant, the question is not whether customers use AI, but whether it increases customer lifetime value by improving retention, engagement and expansion.
Before funding a major investment, I identify the single load-bearing assumption. In our case, it is that customers who incorporate conversational workflows into their daily operations become materially less likely to churn than customers who continue using traditional dashboards alone. I then quantify the expected improvement in customer lifetime value against the engineering and operating investment required.
I would kill the investment if that assumption proves false. For example, if after twelve months AI-powered customers demonstrate higher engagement but no statistically meaningful improvement in retention, renewal or customer lifetime value, I would stop investing in expanding AI capabilities and redirect funding elsewhere. Increased activity without measurable commercial impact is insufficient justification for continued investment.
- **AI. Where do you let AI lead, where a human signs off, and what you never hand over?:** I let AI lead wherever speed, synthesis and natural language understanding create customer value, but I do not let AI make decisions that require business accountability.
Within the MyCF AI Assistant, AI should interpret customer intent, retrieve relevant fleet information, explain trends, summarise operational risks and recommend actions. A human customer ultimately decides what action to take, while MCF remains accountable for ensuring every recommendation is grounded in trusted platform data.
I would never allow the AI Assistant to fabricate answers, make unsupported operational recommendations, or act autonomously on behalf of customers without explicit confirmation. Every response must be evidence-backed, reference the underlying fleet data where appropriate, and clearly distinguish facts from recommendations. Trust is our competitive advantage, so preserving that trust is more important than maximising automation.

## Teach it the people you answer to
- **Who they are and their role:** Head of Product – accountable for the MyCF product portfolio and investment decisions. Owns portfolio priorities, strategic alignment and delivery outcomes across product teams.

Head of Data Science – responsible for the AI architecture, machine learning capabilities and technical approach that underpin the AI Assistant.
- **What they care about most:** Head of Product: Delivering the company strategy, measurable customer and business outcomes, adoption across the platform, and ensuring engineering investment is focused on the highest-value opportunities.

Head of Data Science: Building a scalable, secure and maintainable AI platform, ensuring technical quality, model reliability and architectural sustainability.
- **What they tend to push back on:** Head of Product: Features that are interesting but not strategic, initiatives without measurable success metrics, roadmap changes driven by individual customer requests, or proposals that dilute strategic focus.

Head of Data Science: Product commitments that outpace architectural readiness, solutions that increase technical debt, or timelines that compromise scalability and maintainability.
- **What earns their yes:** Head of Product: A clear strategic rationale, evidence from customer research and usage data, quantified business impact (retention, engagement, CLV), and a realistic delivery plan with explicit trade-offs.

Head of Data Science:  Agreement on the product outcomes first, explicit acknowledgement of genuine technical constraints, and a jointly owned plan for how the architecture evolves to enable the roadmap rather than redefine it.
