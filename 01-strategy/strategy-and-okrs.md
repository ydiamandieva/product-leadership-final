## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration**: Transform MyCF from a system customers use into the operational system they depend on by making conversation the primary interface to fleet intelligence. Our strategic hypothesis is that fleet managers will increasingly choose conversational interaction as their primary operational workflow because it reduces the time and cognitive effort required to make trusted fleet decisions. If this hypothesis proves true, the AI Assistant will increase customer retention, platform engagement and customer lifetime value by embedding MyCF into customers' daily operational decisions rather than occasional reporting workflows. Unlike a feature designed to increase adoption, the AI Assistant is a strategic capability whose purpose is to make MyCF progressively more indispensable after onboarding. |
| **Where to play**: segment, geography, channel, use case (the no's matter too) | Existing MyCF customers who manage fleets within the platform and have sufficient operational data to support trusted, evidence-backed operational answers. We compete inside authenticated workflows where proprietary fleet data creates differentiated value. We deliberately sequence breadth before depth. Phase 1 delivers universal capabilities valuable across every fleet segment, followed by HGV-specific operational workflows in H2 2027. Geography-specific capabilities remain intentionally deferred until after 2027 so that platform-wide value is maximised before investing in localisation. |
| **How to win**: your differentiator competitors can't easily replicate | How to win We become the fastest and most trusted operational interface for fleet managers. Customers should reach for the AI Assistant before navigating reports, dashboards or documentation because it consistently provides faster, evidence-backed answers grounded in their own fleet data while reducing the cognitive effort required to make operational decisions. Over time, the assistant evolves from answering questions to proactively identifying operational opportunities, risks and recommendations. The competitive advantage is not conversational AI itself. The competitive advantage is making MyCF the easiest place to convert proprietary operational data into trusted business decisions. Strategic hypothesis This strategy is based on one central hypothesis: fleet managers will increasingly adopt conversational interaction as their preferred operational workflow when it consistently reduces the time and cognitive effort required to make trusted decisions. As this behaviour becomes habitual, MyCF will become more deeply embedded in customers' daily operations, increasing engagement, customer retention and customer lifetime value. Our management system will continuously test this hypothesis through behavioural adoption, customer value and commercial outcomes. |
| **Capabilities required**: what you must be world-class at (build / buy / partner) | Build Capabilities that create strategic differentiation. Proprietary fleet data integration Domain-specific retrieval (RAG) Embedded conversational experience Explainability and evidence citations AI evaluation framework Feedback learning loops Enterprise governance Operational intelligence layer Buy Commodity capabilities that are not sources of competitive advantage. Foundation models Vector databases Model orchestration Cloud AI infrastructure Monitoring platforms Partner Capabilities accelerated through ecosystem relationships. Cloud providers Frontier AI providers Trusted third-party fleet data Regulatory and mapping providers where they increase answer quality. |
| **Management systems**: the metrics and rituals that reinforce your choices | Success is measured by business outcomes rather than AI activity. We govern through: Product telemetry linked to retention AI quality scorecards Cohort analysis of AI users versus non-users Human and automated evaluation Controlled experimentation Customer feedback loops Governance for latency, hallucination, cost and trust Quarterly strategy reviews against commercial KPIs. |

## 2. Your one hard no

_One valuable thing you are explicitly choosing **not** to do, and why it protects the focus of everything above. This is a deliberate trade-off, not a backlog of deprioritized items._

> We will not answer questions unsupported by MyCF data because our competitive advantage is trusted, evidence-backed fleet intelligence—not generic AI. This protects customer trust, reinforces our differentiation, and keeps every investment focused on increasing retention through reliable operational value.

## 3. OKR cascade

_One Objective and three Key Results that flow directly from the cascade. Each KR must be a measurable **outcome**, not an output/milestone._

> **Objective:**  Increase customer lifetime value by making MyCF the indispensable operational interface customers rely on daily for fleet decisions. 
>
> - **KR1:** Increase annual customer retention from 88% to 92% by 31 December 2027
> - **KR2:** Increase Net Revenue Retention from 102% to 110% by 31 December 2027
> - **KR3:** Increase average Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) by 20% by December 2027

## 4. AI pressure-test notes

_Run the devil's-advocate prompt (in the Sprint 2 guide). Capture the verdict._

| Prompt question | What the AI surfaced | Change or defend? |
|---|---|---|
| Biggest assumption that could be wrong | That increased AI Assistant adoption will directly lead to higher customer retention. Customers may use the assistant frequently without becoming meaningfully more loyal to MyCF. | Change. Strengthen the strategy by making the causal chain explicit: trusted answers → increased workflow dependency → higher engagement → improved retention. Add behavioural metrics that demonstrate AI is changing how customers use MyCF, not just that they use the assistant. |
| The board question I can't yet answer | What evidence do we have that the AI Assistant will improve retention enough to justify the investment? | Change. Define a validation plan using cohort analysis, comparing retention, engagement and customer health scores between AI adopters and non-adopters before scaling investment. |
| KRs that are outputs in disguise | Weekly AI usage and AI adoption could become vanity metrics if treated as success on their own. | Change. Replace or supplement adoption metrics with outcome-oriented measures such as reduction in time to complete operational tasks, percentage of workflows initiated through AI, support ticket reduction and retention uplift. |
| The "no" I should reconsider | Limiting the assistant to MyCF-supported data could frustrate users who expect broader AI capabilities. | Defend. Maintaining this boundary protects trust, accuracy and differentiation. Rather than answering unsupported questions, the assistant should clearly state its limits and redirect users appropriately, reinforcing credibility instead of risking unreliable responses. |
| Strategy or wish list? Why? | Strategy. The Playing to Win cascade defines a clear aspiration, target market, competitive advantage, deliberate trade-off and required capabilities. The hard "no" creates focus, and the OKRs connect product outcomes to business outcomes, making the strategy actionable rather than a collection of desirable features. | Defend. The only refinement is to strengthen the evidence linking AI adoption to retention, not to change the strategic direction. |
