## Make your evaluation and funding decision
- **What assumption is doing the most work? If this number is 20-30% off, what changes?:** The assumption that behavioural adoption of the AI Assistant will materially improve commercial outcomes. Specifically, the strategy assumes that making conversation the primary workflow will increase annual retention from 88% to 92%, NRR from 102% to 110%, and CLV by 20% by December 2027. If behavioural adoption or its impact on renewals is 20–30% lower than expected, these commercial improvements are unlikely to be achieved, significantly weakening the investment case.
- **What is the structural problem in this case? Look past the headline numbers for something that does not hold up on closer inspection.:** The strategy establishes a strong causal narrative but not yet a complete financial one. It links AI adoption to retention, NRR and CLV, but it does not quantify the relationship between them. For example, it doesn't state what percentage of customers must adopt the AI Assistant, how much retention must improve among AI users versus non-users, or how those improvements translate into incremental revenue. Without those assumptions, the business case is difficult to validate financially.
- **Is the kill criterion complete and actionable? Does it name the consequence, or hand the decision back to the room?:** No. There is currently no explicit kill criterion. The strategy explains how success will be measured, but it does not specify when funding should stop or the initiative should pivot. An actionable criterion would be: "If, after six months of General Availability, fewer than 30% of eligible customers use the AI Assistant weekly or AI users do not demonstrate at least a 2 percentage point improvement in retention versus comparable non-users, pause further investment beyond core maintenance and reassess the strategy." This defines a metric, timeframe and consequence.
- **Your verdict: FUND / FUND WITH ONE CONDITION / DO NOT FUND. If a condition, name it; otherwise explain in one sentence.:** FUND WITH ONE CONDITION. Condition: Before additional funding is approved, produce a quantified business case linking behavioural adoption to commercial outcomes, including adoption assumptions, implementation costs, expected revenue impact, sensitivity analysis, and explicit kill criteria.

## Write your business case
- **The strategic bet. What specific outcome are you backing, who does it serve, and what is the mechanism that connects the product decision to a financial result?:** We are betting that existing MyCF fleet customers will adopt conversational interaction as their primary operational workflow because it provides faster, evidence-backed answers with less cognitive effort than navigating reports and dashboards. By embedding the AI Assistant into customers' daily operational decisions, MyCF becomes progressively more indispensable after onboarding, increasing customer engagement, reducing churn, improving expansion opportunities and ultimately increasing Customer Lifetime Value (CLV). The strategic value is not AI itself, but strengthening customer dependency on proprietary fleet intelligence that competitors cannot easily replicate.
- **The assumptions. List the assumptions your case rests on, then rank them: which one, if wrong, most changes your conclusion?:** 1. (Critical) Fleet managers will repeatedly choose conversational interaction over existing navigation-based workflows when answers are trustworthy and materially faster.
2. MyCF data is sufficiently complete and accurate to generate trusted operational answers across priority use cases.
3. Increased engagement translates into higher retention and Net Revenue Retention.
4. Customers perceive explainability and evidence citations as sufficient to trust AI-generated operational answers.
5. The cost of delivering AI responses remains economically sustainable as adoption scales.
6. Competitors cannot rapidly replicate our differentiated experience because our advantage is rooted in proprietary fleet data rather than conversational AI alone.

Most critical assumption:
The business case depends primarily on customers changing their operational behaviour. If conversational interaction does not become a habitual workflow, increased retention and Customer Lifetime Value will not follow, regardless of technical execution.

4. Kill Criterion
Strategic investment should stop when evidence demonstrates that conversational interaction does not improve customer economics.
Terminate further strategic investment in expanding AI Assistant capabilities if, within 12 months of General Availability:
fewer than 30% of eligible customer accounts demonstrate sustained weekly operational use of the AI Assistant;
cohort analysis shows no statistically significant improvement in Annual Customer Retention or Net Revenue Retention compared with comparable non-adopting customers;
projected Customer Lifetime Value uplift is insufficient to maintain an LTV:CAC ratio above 3:1; or
cumulative incremental CLV generated by AI-enabled customers is not expected to exceed cumulative AI operating costs within the agreed investment horizon.
If any of these conditions are met, the strategic hypothesis that conversational interaction creates durable commercial value is considered disproven. Investment should be limited to maintenance and contractual obligations, with no further strategic expansion until a revised business case demonstrates an acceptable financial return.

From a finance perspective, the investment thesis is:
We are investing in a strategic capability that increases Customer Lifetime Value by making MyCF indispensable to customers' daily operations. Success is measured not by AI usage, but by superior customer economics that generate an LTV:CAC ratio above 3:1 and recover investment within the company's acceptable payback period.
